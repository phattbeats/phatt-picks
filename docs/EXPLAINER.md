# HOTLINE, Explained

*A plain-English guide to what this app is, why it exists, and how everything in it works. No technical background needed — read it top to bottom. There's a [glossary](#glossary) at the end for any unfamiliar word.*

> If you only read one paragraph: **HOTLINE is a private prediction game for a group of friends who watch professional Counter-Strike.** Before each big tournament, everyone guesses which teams will do well. The app keeps score automatically, hides everyone's guesses until it's too late to copy, and then reveals them all at once so you can see who called it and who whiffed. It's a season-long bragging-rights contest with your mates.

## 1. The short version

HOTLINE is a website (that also installs on your phone like an app) where a small group of friends predict the results of professional **Counter-Strike** tournaments and compete to be the most accurate.

The loop is simple:

1. A big tournament is coming up.
2. Each person logs in and makes their **predictions** — which teams will advance, which will get knocked out, who wins it all.
3. Everyone's predictions are **kept secret** until the deadline passes (so nobody can peek and copy).
4. The matches happen in real life. HOTLINE watches the real results and **scores everyone automatically**.
5. The **leaderboard** shows who's winning. At the end you get a flashy recap of how your tournament went.

That's it. Everything else in the app is polish on top of that core idea: making it prettier, more social, more fun, and zero-effort to keep score.

## 2. The backstory — why this exists

Valve — the company that makes Counter-Strike — runs an official prediction game during big tournaments, called the **Pick'Em Challenge**. It's fun, but it lives inside Steam and it's a bit clunky for a friend group that just wants to talk trash and see a shared scoreboard.

HOTLINE was built to be the **friend-group layer on top of that**. It can pull in your official Valve picks, or you can just play inside HOTLINE without touching Steam at all. Either way, everyone in the group lands on **one shared leaderboard** with a clean, modern interface, recaps, reactions, and reminders — the stuff Valve's version doesn't give you.

It's deliberately **private and small** — it's for one specific group, not the public. And it's built to **come back every tournament**: when the next Major rolls around, the same app re-points itself at the new event and everyone plays again, with the old seasons kept in the history.

## 3. Counter-Strike and "Majors," for the uninitiated

You don't need to know anything about the game to use HOTLINE, but a little context makes the picks make sense.

- **Counter-Strike 2 (CS2)** is a hugely popular competitive video game. Two teams of five pros play rounds against each other; first to a target number of rounds wins the map.
- A **professional match** is usually a "best of three" (often shortened to **BO3**) — first team to win two maps wins the match. Some matches are a single map (**BO1**) or best of five (**BO5**) for finals.
- A **Major** is the Counter-Strike world championship — the biggest, most prestigious tournament of the season, sponsored by Valve. The best teams in the world — a field of **32** — compete over a couple of weeks for the trophy and a huge prize pool, with the field narrowing stage by stage.
- HOTLINE's first full season ran on **IEM Cologne 2026** (June 2026), which has now **wrapped up** — that tournament is in the books and archived. The next Major already lined up is **PGL Singapore 2026** (later in 2026), which the group will play when it comes around.

When HOTLINE asks you to "pick teams," it's asking you to predict how these real pro teams will do at the real tournament.

## 4. What a "Pick'Em" is

A **Pick'Em** is a prediction game. Before the tournament starts, you "pick 'em" — you choose which teams you think will do well — and you earn points for being right.

You're **not** picking the winner of every single match. Instead, at each stage of the tournament, you answer a few broad questions like:

- *Which teams will breeze through this stage without losing?*
- *Which teams will scrape through?*
- *Which teams will crash out early?*
- *And eventually: who lifts the trophy?*

You lock in your answers **before** that stage begins. Then you wait, watch, and see how many you got right. Simple to play, surprisingly hard to nail.

## 5. How a tournament is shaped (Swiss stages + playoffs)

A CS2 Major happens in two big phases. Understanding the shape makes the picks obvious.

### The Swiss stages (the group phase)

Early on, teams play in what's called a **Swiss** format. Don't overthink the name — here's all it means:

- A pile of teams all start at 0 wins, 0 losses.
- Each round, teams with **similar records play each other** (the unbeaten teams play other unbeaten teams; the strugglers play other strugglers).
- **Win 3 matches and you advance.** **Lose 3 matches and you're out.** Everyone else keeps playing until they hit one of those.

So in any Swiss stage, every team ends in one of three boxes:

| Box | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Advanced 3–0** | Sailed through without a loss — the strongest performers. |
| **Advanced** | Made it through, but dropped a game or two along the way. |
| **Eliminated 0–3** | Knocked out without a win — the disappointments. |

When HOTLINE shows you a Swiss stage to pick, it asks you to **sort the teams into those three boxes**: who goes 3–0, who advances the hard way, who busts out 0–3. (Internally the app calls this the **"2 / 6 / 2" buckets** — two teams in the top box, six in the middle, two in the bottom — but you just drag teams where you think they belong.)

A Major usually has a few of these Swiss stages back to back, each with a different set of teams, narrowing the field down.

### The playoffs (the bracket)

Once the Swiss stages have sorted out who's good, the survivors go into a **single-elimination bracket** — lose once and you're done. This is the dramatic end of the tournament:

- **Quarter-finals (QF)** → **Semi-finals (SF)** → **Grand Final (GF)**.
- Win your match, advance to the next round. Lose, go home.

In HOTLINE, the playoffs are **one interactive bracket** you fill out in a single sitting: you tap the team you think wins each quarter-final, and they automatically advance to the semis, where you pick again, and so on up to crowning your champion. It's the classic "fill out your bracket" experience.

## 6. How you actually play HOTLINE, step by step

### Step 1 — Get in

There are two ways to log in:

- **Sign in with Steam.** This links your real Steam account. The upside: HOTLINE can read your official Valve Pick'Em picks and (if you want) push your HOTLINE picks back up to Valve, so you only have to decide once.
- **Play locally (no Steam needed).** One tap creates a guest player that lives only inside HOTLINE. Perfect for friends who don't want to mess with Steam. You can link Steam later and bring your guest picks with you.

Either way you end up as a **player** with a profile, an avatar, and a spot on the leaderboard.

### Step 2 — Make your picks

Go to the **Picks** page. Depending on what stage the tournament is in, you'll either:

- **Sort teams into the three Swiss boxes** (drag a team, or tap it, into "3–0," "advances," or "0–3"), or
- **Fill out the playoff bracket** (tap your winner in each matchup and watch them advance to the next round).

You can change your mind as much as you like — **until the deadline**.

### Step 3 — Lock in

Every stage has a **lock time** — a deadline that lines up with when the real matches begin. Before the deadline you can edit freely. The app shows you whether your picks are **saved** (a green "✓ Saved" / "Synced" indicator) or whether you have unsaved edits (a yellow indicator nudging you to lock them in).

Once the deadline passes, that stage is **locked**: your picks are frozen and can't be changed. This is what makes the game fair — everybody commits before anyone knows what happens.

### Step 4 (optional) — Sync to Steam

If you signed in with Steam, you can **mirror** your picks:

- **Pull from Valve** — import whatever you already set in the official Steam Pick'Em.
- **Push to Valve** — send your HOTLINE picks up to become your official Steam picks. (This is off by default during normal play and only the owner enables it, because it overwrites your live Valve picks — it's a deliberate, guarded action.)

If you play locally, you skip this entirely — your picks just live in HOTLINE.

## 7. How scoring works

You don't keep score yourself — HOTLINE does it automatically as real results come in.

The key ideas:

- **You earn points for correct calls.** If you said a team would go 3–0 and they did, that's points. If you said they'd bust out and they did, points.
- **HOTLINE uses Valve's own point values** — the same weighting the real Steam Pick'Em uses, read straight from Valve's tournament data, not invented by the app.
- **Later stages are worth more.** Each Swiss stage has a flat value per correct pick that climbs as the tournament deepens: **1 point each in Stage I, 2 in Stage II, 3 in Stage III** (60 points up for grabs across the Swiss stages). Within a single stage, every correct call is worth the same — there's **no bonus for picking an upset over a favorite**; you just have to be right.
- **Swiss picks are about the box, not the exact match.** You don't have to predict every single match result — you just have to get a team into the **right box** (advanced / 3–0 / eliminated). If you put a team in the "advances" box and they advance, you're right, however they got there.
- **The playoff bracket** scores each match you call correctly: **12 points for a quarter-final, 10 for a semi-final, 7 for the grand final** (75 points across the playoffs). A flawless tournament — every Swiss pick and every bracket match — is worth **135 points**.

The result is a single point total per player that drives the leaderboard. No spreadsheets, no manual tallying.

## 8. The reveal — why your picks are hidden

This is the heart of what makes HOTLINE fun and fair: **nobody can see anyone else's picks until the stage locks.**

- While a stage is still open, your picks are **private**. You can't scout what your friends did, and they can't copy you.
- The moment a stage **locks** (deadline passes), the picks for that stage **flip to public** — and HOTLINE gives you a **Reveal** board showing everyone's calls side by side.

So the rhythm of each stage is: *everyone commits in secret → the deadline hits → the big reveal → trash talk → the matches play out → scores land.* The hidden-then-revealed flip is enforced everywhere at once, so picks never leak early.

## 9. The leaderboard and comparing

- The **home screen** greets you with a quick-look snapshot: the current stage, the top few players, and where you sit — so you see the state of play the moment you open the app.
- The **Leaderboard** is the full shared scoreboard — every player ranked by total points. This is the bragging-rights centerpiece.
- **Compare** lets you put your picks head-to-head against a specific friend, stage by stage, to see exactly where you agreed, where you split, and who came out ahead.
- The app also surfaces fun details like a **"lone pick"** — when you were the *only* person brave (or foolish) enough to call a particular result.

## 10. The fun extras

These are the features that make HOTLINE more than a scoreboard. None of them are required to play — they're the flavor.

- **Stage Wrapped** — a slick, click-through recap deck (think "Spotify Wrapped" but for the tournament stage). After a stage resolves, you can **replay** an animated slideshow of how it went — your hits, your misses, the big storylines, fun stats, even an optional soundtrack — from the Reveal board. (Stage recaps open on demand, not automatically.) The one exception is the grand **Playoffs Wrapped** finale: when the Major crowns its champion, that championship recap pops up on its own the next time you open the app.

- **The Bleachers** — lightweight social reactions. On a friend's **revealed** pick, you can drop one fixed **reaction stamp** from a small set. Three are respect — **🔥 Fire**, **🎯 Called it**, **🧠 Bold** — and two are friendly jabs — **🧊 Ice cold** and **🗿 Cope**. You get one stamp per pick (dropping a new one swaps the old). In the moment the reactions are **anonymous**, so you can chirp someone's terrible pick without immediately outing yourself; when the stage **resolves**, the masks come off and everyone sees who reacted to what.

- **Challenge Coins** — collectible commemorative coins, one per Major you take part in. The **tier** is set by where you finish: **diamond** for the top 10% (the outright winner always gets diamond), **gold** for the top 25%, **silver** for the top 50%, and **bronze** for everyone else who took part. You earn a coin once the Major wraps up, and they're displayed in a little 3D **display case** on your profile that you can spin and inspect. They're a keepsake of each season you played.

- **Playoff Spotlight** — for the playoff teams, a per-team narrative and highlight panel. When a matchup's market is available, it also shows **live betting-market odds** (pulled from a public prediction market) so you can see who the world thinks will win; until those are wired up for a matchup, the panel just says the odds are coming soon.

- **News** — a lightweight in-app "wire" for tournament updates. It only shows real, curated items — when there's nothing to report it honestly says so rather than padding the feed.

- **Player profiles** — every player has a profile page showing their avatar, their current **rank and points** (broken down stage by stage), their **Challenge Coin** case, the reaction stamps left on their picks, and their full pick history once each stage has revealed. Your own **"Your Majors"** page collects your finish and coin from every Major you've played.

## 11. Notifications and reminders

HOTLINE can nudge you so you never miss a deadline:

- **Pre-lock reminders** — opt-in pings **24 hours and 1 hour** before each stage locks, so you have time to get your picks in.
- **Real-time notifications** — when a stage is about to lock, when reactions land on your picks, when a stage recap is ready, when you earn a Challenge Coin, and the occasional broadcast announcement. There's an in-app inbox (the bell), and the app shows an unread badge. You can mark items read, mark all read, and filter to just the unread ones.
- You control all of this under your settings — turn reminders on, send yourself a test, or switch things off.

On a phone, these arrive as real push notifications (see the next section for the one catch on iPhone).

## 12. Putting it on your phone

HOTLINE is a **PWA** — a "progressive web app." In plain terms: it's a website that you can **install to your home screen** so it behaves like a normal app (its own icon, full screen, no browser bars).

- **iPhone:** open HOTLINE in Safari, tap **Share → Add to Home Screen**, then launch it from the new icon. On iPhone this step is **required** if you want push notifications — Apple only delivers them to an installed home-screen app.
- **Android / desktop:** use the browser's **"Install app"** option. Notifications work even without installing, but installing is nicer.

There's no app store, no download — it's just the website, saved to your home screen.

## 13. Inviting friends

Every player has a personal **invite link** (it looks like `…/join/<code>`). Share it from your settings (**Invite friends**), and whoever clicks it gets onboarded in a single tap — they can start playing locally with no Steam account required. That's how the group grows.

## 14. Behind the scenes (for the curious)

You can skip this section entirely and still use the app perfectly. It's here for anyone who wants to know how the sausage is made, in non-technical terms.

- **It's a single, self-contained app.** HOTLINE runs as one program on a home server (an Unraid box on the PHATT network), reachable on the internet at `pickems.phatt.vip`. There's a reverse proxy (**SWAG**) sitting in front of it that handles the secure `https://` address.

- **Its memory is a small database.** Everyone's picks, scores, profiles, coins, and reactions live in a lightweight database file (**SQLite**) stored on the server's disk. That's the app's long-term memory.

- **Where the real results come from.** HOTLINE doesn't guess the scores — it reads them from authoritative sources:
  - **Valve** (via the official Steam Web API) is the ultimate "answer key" — once Valve marks the official results of a stage, HOTLINE uses those to score everyone.
  - **HLTV** (the main Counter-Strike results site) is read for *live* mid-stage standings, because Valve doesn't publish win-loss records while a stage is still in progress. HLTV sits behind an anti-bot wall, so the app fetches those pages through a helper service called **crawl4ai**.
  - **Liquipedia** (a community esports wiki) is used for match outcomes as well.

- **The crawl4ai helper is optional.** It only powers the *live, in-progress* standings and the "last 5 games" team form panels. If it's ever unavailable, those two features just show empty or slightly stale data — **everything else (logging in, making picks, scoring, the leaderboard, reveals, recaps, reactions) keeps working normally.** It is not something the app needs to run.

- **Deploys.** When the app is updated, the owner pulls the new version and the server swaps it in. The database survives the swap, so nobody loses their picks or scores.

If you want the deep technical version, the project keeps a developer knowledge base in its `docs/` folder (architecture, operations, the runbook for standing up the next Major, and a list of hard-won gotchas). This explainer is the friendly front door to all of that. And if you ever want to run your **own** copy of HOTLINE, the **[Admin & Operator Guide](ADMIN-GUIDE.md)** walks you through deploying it on your own hardware, start to finish.

## 15. What happens between tournaments

HOTLINE is built to **come back for every Major**, not to be thrown away after one.

- The app is **generic by design** — when a new Major is announced, a small amount of configuration re-points it at the new event (the teams, the schedule, the stages), and the whole group plays again from scratch. With **IEM Cologne 2026** now finished, the next event, **PGL Singapore 2026**, is already lined up in the app for when it rolls around later in the year.
- Past seasons aren't deleted — they're **archived**. Cologne 2026 is now the first season in that archive, so you can look back at its leaderboard, and your **Challenge Coins** from it (and every Major after) stay in your display case as a record of every tournament you played.

So it's less of a one-off and more of an ongoing, season-after-season tradition for the group.
